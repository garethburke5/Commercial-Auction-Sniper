Status: owner feedback recorded; implementation and specimen reissue outstanding.
This document is not a paid-launch approval or evidence of completed implementation.

## Shared product standard

These requirements apply to the reusable Full Investment Report feature, not only
a rewritten example. Preserve the meaningful legal and commercial analysis in the
reviewed Surbiton report. Free snapshots remain listing-data products with
deterministic calculations and no paid generation on request.

## First-page transaction overview

Clearly title the product Full Investment Report. Present a compact summary of:
latest captured guide; reported/achieved sale and its evidence status; current
annual passing rent; gross initial yield and denominator; gross return after
identified acquisition costs; tenant; lease expiry; next break and conditions;
rent review arrangements; tenure and exact interest; VAT; deposit; completion;
identified buyer costs above the price and total acquisition expenditure.

Use “Not confirmed” for unresolved fields. Do not label gross income divided by
acquisition expenditure as net yield. Distinguish identified amounts, tax
illustrations and unpriced costs. A deposit is part of the price, not an extra
expense. Do not carry a deposit calculated at the guide across to a different
sale price without recalculating from an evidenced contractual requirement.

## Ownership and retained liabilities

Explain what the purchaser owns, the occupied/leased interests, exclusions,
income entitlement and retained building obligations. Distinguish proposed
long leases from executed grants; an auctioneer's description does not establish
execution. Use a concise ownership table.

Retain material title-reference, demise-plan and basement-extent findings.
Do not describe incomplete drafts as proof of defective registered ownership.
A photograph's yellow outline is illustrative and must not imply that excluded
upper accommodation or its rental income belongs to the purchaser.

## Prioritised findings

Use a concise Finding | Financial/legal implication | Required clarification
table. Keep material income, expenditure, security and ownership issues prominent.
Preserve confirmed / potential / unresolved / routine distinctions.
Avoid duplicating long prose from that table elsewhere.

Consider conflicting uplift dates, enforceable seller top-ups, retained structural
costs, incomplete title documents, referenced-but-missing compliance documents,
tenant security, VAT and insurance where the evidence supports them.
Do not manufacture findings to populate a fixed number of rows.

## Lease and seller-enquiry interpretation

Explicitly address:
- Landlord and Tenant Act 1954 status, including evidence of valid contracting out.
- Internal/demised repair obligations versus retained structure; do not call every
  service-charge lease fully repairing and insuring.
- Who insures, what each tenant pays, allocation/excesses and remaining exposure.
- Actual rent deposit, guarantor or security. Assignment clauses contemplating
  future security are not evidence that present security exists.
- Disclosed arrears, rent-free periods, concessions and their dates.
- Service-charge scope, cap, indexation, expiry and excluded/unrecoverable costs.
- Actual Commercial Property Standard Enquiries replies and qualified answers.

Read the supplied documents first. Request clarification only for material gaps
not already answered. Missing supporting documents are not proof of a breach,
positive asbestos finding or an uninsured building.

## Financial sensitivity and principal insight

Replace rent-only vacancy arithmetic as the central analysis with annual
unrecovered-cost scenarios of £5,000 and £10,000; a separately labelled substantial
one-off repair; prolonged vacancy including continuing ownership costs; and
higher rent only as a conditional scenario where evidenced.

Show period, rent received, recurring costs, one-off cost, remaining cash and
return on a consistent identified acquisition denominator. Costs represent the
landlord's balance after recoveries; never subtract a cap from a gross invoice
to invent recoveries. Preserve negative cash. Exclude unspecified finance and
investor tax explicitly. Do not present scenarios as forecasts or net yields.

Each paid report should prominently explain at least one supported, material
insight beyond the particulars, and its investment consequence. Lack of a
supported insight is a product-quality issue, not permission to invent one.

## Presentation and evidence

Aim for 3–5 pages, including consolidated evidence. Additional length needs a
material reason. Use restrained navy/teal styling, efficient first-page space and
selective tables. Retain useful market evidence; avoid decorative graphics.

All full references stay together at the end. Optional small numbered references
may connect findings to sources. Verify public hyperlink destinations; verify
private document names, clause numbers and physical PDF pages; distinguish draft
and executed documents; make important findings individually traceable.
Do not invent public/private evidence links or imply access permission.
