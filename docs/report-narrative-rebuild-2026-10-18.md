# Report narrative rebuild following the owner's rejection

The earlier Newbury reports remain rejected. The release gate for £19 reports stays closed. This change does not record owner or independent commercial approval.

## Actual process change

A completed evidence investigation now feeds a separate writing stage and then a paragraph-by-paragraph editorial stage. These use the same bounded provider instance and usage ledger as the investigation. The writer receives the property identity, price basis, income, leases, material findings, market records, arithmetic, evidence and uncertainties. It writes complete sections instead of selecting fixed customer paragraphs.

`acquisition_narrative.py` binds the written assessment to the precise evidence and financial context. Changed properties, rents, prices or source content invalidate a saved draft. Every paragraph identifies its facts; every used fact must have a contextual source somewhere in the report. Prior asking prices and material findings are required coverage. Unknown facts, unsupported numeric tokens, missing sections and extraction artefacts fail the contract. Those checks do not prove semantic support or good prose: the separate editor must check what each paragraph actually says, including tagged-but-unexplained issues. Its result is bound to both the evidence and exact draft. Any failed review keeps the result held.

The normal PDF, HTML and DOCX renderer consumes the written sections while retaining calculated income, lease-date, comparison and tenant-loss charts. Old reports remain readable. A missing or invalid new writer result cannot silently become a finished snapshot. Incomplete pack evidence retains the research-update product; it does not become a full report merely because a writer supplied prose.

## Reusable research fixes

The research brief explicitly seeks previous asking prices, listing dates, the ownership being offered and tenancy changes. A listing-added date is separate from the price observed at retrieval. The public research context includes commercial legal tenants but excludes residential tenants' identities. Business names, individual tenants, company accounts and evidence of actual payments must be distinguished. No private personal background, health or residential-contact investigation is requested.

The source-bound public rent-review reconciliation accepts only an explicitly settled and documented whole-property rent review tied to the superseded subject record. It does not override private leases, proposals, another lot, one unit within a multi-let property or competing revised amounts.

## New specimens and what they demonstrate

- Newbury full review: five main pages and a further-checks appendix. Natural opening with £26,700 stated rent, £190,000 auctioneer-reported sale and 14.05% gross yield; distinct guide; earlier £265,000 ask; individual main tenant versus the café trading name; conditional break and lost-rent implications; ownership, energy, insurance and repair issues; relevant but qualified investment comparisons; real fee clauses and historical residential expenditure.
- Newbury free snapshot: one page, public evidence only, including price history. It does not borrow private lease findings.
- 29/30 Jansel Square, Aylesbury: a genuinely new public-evidence case. The brochure's £30,000 rent and £350,000 guide produced its 8.6% headline. The subject-matched addendum expressly records the review at £36,000; the auctioneer reports a £516,000 sale. The shared engine starts from the old rent and applies that documented amendment. The resulting gross yield is 6.98%. All parts are let to one tenant; the flat is not treated as an additional residential rent. This case is now a regression case, not an untouched future holdout.

The specimens use retained Newbury evidence and freshly captured primary marketing/business sources. Their narratives were composed and edited by the assistant in this work session, then validated and rendered through the production contract. No OpenAI API calls or credits were used. They do **not** prove the live provider independently produces equally good writing. The full enlarged Newbury evidence set has not undergone a fresh independent source audit.

## Editorial review and remaining acceptance

Each specimen paragraph was checked for claim support, understandable terminology, chronology and price basis, conditional rights, relevance and a useful consequence. This caught the missing prior ask, the distinction between the legal tenant and trading name, the risk of interpreting a listing date as a dated price, the residential scope of the £4,150 expenditure and an incorrectly adjacent historical-cost source. Layout review corrected a source overflow and removed repeated source lists from the overview/conclusion while retaining references beside detailed findings.

Remaining work before paid approval: an uncoached complete report from an unfamiliar legal pack; stronger property-specific rent/letting and works evidence where available; live writer/editor performance and measured cost; the full customer purchase/credit, delivery, re-access and insufficient-evidence journey. A public-only case cannot prove full legal-pack reporting. Automated tests, deployment and these editorial specimens are evidence of implementation, not a £19 quality certificate.
